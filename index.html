Environmental & Human Impacts

🐟 Wildlife & Ecosystems

Artificial light at night (ALAN) disrupts the natural rhythms of life that evolved under darkness for billions of years.

  • Sea turtles — Hatchlings crawl toward artificial lights instead of the moonlit ocean; millions die each year.
  • Migratory birds — Disoriented by skyglow and building lights; massive collision mortality.
  • Insects — Attracted to lights in fatal numbers; pollination and food webs suffer (studies suggest caterpillar declines of ~50% in lit areas).
  • Bats, amphibians, coral — Foraging, reproduction and symbiotic relationships are altered.

🧠 Human Health

Light at night suppresses melatonin, the hormone that regulates our circadian clock.

  • Disrupted sleep and circadian rhythms
  • Links to increased risk of obesity, diabetes, depression, and certain cancers (breast, prostate, colorectal)
  • Blue-rich LED light is particularly disruptive

⚡ Energy & Climate

Outdoor and indoor lighting accounts for roughly 15–20% of global electricity use and contributes several percent of CO₂ emissions. Much of this light is wasted upward into the sky.

🌌 The Night Sky

One-third of humanity can no longer see the Milky Way. Star visibility is declining 7–10% per year in many places. Cultural heritage, astronomy, and simple wonder are being lost.

Solutions for Darker Nights

Light pollution is one of the easiest environmental problems to reduce — often with immediate, visible results.

1. Use light only where & when needed

  • Install lighting only for genuine safety or utility needs
  • Use the lowest brightness that still does the job
  • Timers, motion sensors and dimmers dramatically cut waste

2. Shield & direct light downward

  • Fully shielded fixtures prevent light from escaping upward or sideways
  • Aim lights carefully — no light trespass into windows or habitats
  • Mount lights as low as practical

3. Choose warmer, less blue spectra

  • Prefer amber / warm-white LEDs (≤ 2700–3000 K) at night
  • Blue-rich light scatters more and is more biologically disruptive
  • Color-tunable or filtered lighting is ideal

4. Support Dark Sky places & policy

  • Over 320 certified Dark Sky places exist worldwide
  • Advocate for lighting ordinances, responsible urban design, and protected dark corridors
  • Organizations: DarkSky International, IUCN Dark Skies, local astronomy clubs

5. Personal actions

  • Close curtains at night / use blackout if needed
  • Switch outdoor lights to motion-activated or timers
  • Talk to neighbors and local government about better lighting
  • Experience a truly dark sky — it changes how you feel about the night

About This Project

This interactive Digital Earth is an educational experience designed to make the invisible visible: the growing blanket of artificial light that is changing ecosystems, human health, and our connection to the cosmos.

It was built as an open, immersive web experience using Three.js and public Earth observation inspired assets.
